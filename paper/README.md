# Working paper

Read [main.pdf](main.pdf): the consolidated manuscript, historical introduction,
counterexample campaign, and figures (31 pages, including references, in the
September 6 build; 11-point type and one-inch margins).
The global conjecture remains open.

`main.tex` loads the editable files in `sections/` and `references.tex`.
The main manuscript now includes the principal mathematics formerly presented
only in [technical-details.pdf](technical-details.pdf): the reduced kinetic
energy, both escape criteria, near-isosceles variational argument, incoming
matching and parabolic transversality, uniform first-turn exclusion, joint
triple-collision scaling, planar collision certificates, real collision
sampling, and explicit torque thresholds. `sections/returns.tex` contains
the returning-orbit and collision-boundary analysis.

The former 50-page companion remains an expanded research archive. Detailed
coefficient lists and full certificate records are indexed from the main
paper's reproducibility appendix. The accessible opening and historical
narrative are retained. The September 6 revision is titled *The Pythagorean
Three-Body Conjecture*, adds the Shaquille O'Neal epigraph with a linked
attribution, and removes the draft author line. Its abstract is 144
whitespace-delimited words: the first two sentences of the September 5
abstract are unchanged, followed by the explicit certified interval,
brake-event reduction, thin-family density theorem, first-turn exclusion,
and numerical-search status.
The subsequent September 6 revision adds an explicit Hamiltonian counterexample
to the measure-zero inference and an isolated rational periodic-parameter example.
Section 1 closes with a dated, subjective 10% assessment of the existence of an
integer Pythagorean counterexample, under the user-specified attribution
"GPT-6 Astra"; this is not a statistical estimate or a mathematical result.
Section 2 develops the continued F30 near miss and shows its trajectory in
place of the redundant defect plot; all six signed defects remain in Table 1.
The latest opening-page adjustment aligns the epigraph with the abstract's
left margin, adds a line of vertical space above it, and renames Section 1
"A simple plan."
See [the consolidation review](../docs/MANUSCRIPT_EDITING_PASS_2026-09-05.md)
for the material included and the scope of verification.

The pre-review PDF was 35 pages, dated August 24. Its later August 26 LaTeX
source had grown substantially and was no longer represented by that PDF.
Both are preserved in `archive/`; they are historical, uncorrected versions.
See [the review](../docs/MANUSCRIPT_REVIEW_2026-09-05.md) and especially
[the forced-lag correction](../docs/FORCED_LAG_ENDPOINT_CORRECTION.md).

## Build

From the repository root, using Tectonic:

```sh
tectonic --keep-logs paper/main.tex
tectonic --keep-logs paper/technical-details.tex
```

In an environment that requires a writable temporary cache:

```sh
TECTONIC_CACHE_DIR=/private/tmp/burrau-tectonic tectonic --keep-logs paper/main.tex
TECTONIC_CACHE_DIR=/private/tmp/burrau-tectonic tectonic --keep-logs paper/technical-details.tex
```

Alternatively, run `pdflatex` twice on each document from `paper/`.
Tectonic was used for the reviewed build; pdfLaTeX was not available in
that environment. The reviewed `main.pdf` and archived `technical-details.pdf`
are now explicitly tracked for preservation; other top-level generated PDFs
and logs remain ignored. The current main PDF is also deployed on Oklo; see
the [publication record](../docs/OKLO_PUBLICATION_2026-09-06.md).

## Figures

`make_figures.py` generates the two vector PDFs from formulas and
`data/periodic_atlas_campaign.json`. It needs NumPy and Matplotlib; these
are plotting dependencies, separate from the research environment's lock.
For example:

```sh
uv run --no-project --with numpy --with matplotlib python paper/make_figures.py
```

The reviewed figures used Python 3.13, NumPy 2.5.2, and Matplotlib 3.11.1
in an isolated temporary environment. The original fan image was copied
unchanged from `../burrau/plots/oklo_pythagorean_fan_trajectory_progression.png`.
Its adjacent metadata JSON is copied to `figures/fan-provenance.json`.
The caption records interval widths, sampling, opacity, and spatial clipping.
No displayed trajectory is represented as an interval certificate.
Figure 1 was regenerated on September 6 with aligned panel titles and
arrowheads pointing to the labelled curve points. The isosceles endpoint
label is placed inside the circle, clear of its arc. To regenerate only
that figure in the plotting environment, run
`python -c 'from paper.make_figures import geometry; geometry()'` from the
repository root.

The F30 trajectory image was copied without alteration from
`../burrau/plots/f30_mass_side_periodic_candidate.png`, generated by
`../burrau/scripts/plot_f30_mass_side_periodic_candidate.py`.
Its audit is copied to `figures/f30-plot-audit.txt`. The plotting replay has
maximum five-equation residual about 2.6e-11 and constructs the return half by
time reversal; the caption explicitly says that visual closure is not an
independent numerical check. The campaign's separately refined root has the
smaller residuals discussed in the text. Its minimum separation is numerically
resolved at about 5.0e-4, whereas uniform physical-time sampling in the plotting
audit records about 7.45e-4; neither is a certified lower bound. The original
vector defect plot remains available in `figures/counterexample-defects.pdf`.
