\begin{abstract}
We study free-fall solutions of the planar Newtonian three-body problem in
which the masses and their opposite side lengths are the same Pythagorean
triple.  The desired nonperiodicity theorem is not proved in this draft.  We
give exact reductions to primitive triples and a rational algebraic parameter
curve, prove equivalence with absence of a second brake, construct a
syzygy-valid three-component reduced brake residual, derive the universal
zero-angular-momentum reduced system, and verify exact initial identities.  We
also prove that the real isosceles-right endpoint has no second brake and ends
in finite collision. At the opposite singular endpoint, a Levi--Civita
argument proves that every sufficiently skinny member avoids the limiting
heavy-pair collision on its first encounter, with explicit miss-distance and
angular-momentum asymptotics. We derive the later rectilinear restricted
scattering limit and prove that it has a parabolic separatrix, ruling out a
phase-uniform strict-escape shortcut. We derive the exact collapsed-pair phase
clock and the finite-parameter field through second order, exposing a
parabolic time-translation gauge in the candidate splitting integral. A
rigorous interval-Taylor computation proves the remaining finite Jacobi sign
uniformly on an explicit launch-speed interval. An analytic compactification
then verifies McGehee's degenerate stable-manifold hypotheses and proves that
the limiting incoming and outgoing parabolic curves cross transversely. This
auxiliary limiting theorem does not yet provide uniform finite-parameter
matching or exclude brakes on captured branches. A bounded catalog audit finds
a close numerical periodic brake orbit on the
$3{:}4{:}5$ mass slice, but its initial triangle is not the exact tied right
triangle.
\end{abstract}

\section{Exact conjecture}

Let $a^2+b^2=c^2$ with positive integers, set
$(m_1,m_2,m_3)=(a,b,c)$, and put
\[
q_1=(-c/2,0),\quad q_2=(c/2,0),\quad
q_3=((b^2-a^2)/(2c),ab/c),\qquad \dot q_i=0.
\]
The side opposite each mass has the same length as that mass.  The conjecture
asserts that no classical solution has a collision-free labelled period.
Returns modulo rotation or label exchange and regularized continuation after
collision are excluded.

\section{Scaling and Euclid parameter}

\begin{lemma}[Simultaneous mass--length scaling]
If $q_i(t)$ solves the equations with masses $m_i$, then for $k>0$
\[
\widetilde m_i=km_i,\qquad \widetilde q_i(t)=kq_i(t/k)
\]
solves the rescaled problem.  A labelled period $T$ corresponds exactly to a
period $kT$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Both sides of Newton's acceleration equation acquire the factor $k^{-1}$.
Velocity is unchanged after the corresponding time substitution, and mutual
distances acquire the positive factor $k$.
\end{proof}

It follows that only primitive triples matter.  For $u\in(0,1)$ define
\[
A(u)=\frac{1-u^2}{1+u^2},\qquad
B(u)=\frac{2u}{1+u^2}.
\]
If $u=p/q$ is reduced, Euclid's integer triple is
$(q^2-p^2,2pq,q^2+p^2)$ and its common divisor is two exactly when $p,q$ are
both odd.  Conversely Euclid's theorem recovers every ordered primitive
triple.  The inverse is $u=B/(1+A)$, and leg exchange is the involution
$u\mapsto(1-u)/(1+u)$.  Its fixed point is $\sqrt2-1$, giving the fundamental
interval $0<u\leq\sqrt2-1$ for the real family.

After normalizing $c=1$, masses and positions are
\[
(A,B,1),\qquad q_1=(-1/2,0),\quad q_2=(1/2,0),\quad
q_3=((B^2-A^2)/2,AB).
\]

\section{Second brakes and a quotient residual}

\begin{lemma}[Second-brake lemma]
A classical free-fall segment generates a collision-free labelled periodic
solution if and only if all inertial velocities vanish at some positive time
before collision.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Uniqueness and time reversal give $q(-t)=q(t)$.  A brake at $\tau$ gives
$q(\tau+s)=q(\tau-s)$ and hence a labelled period $2\tau$.  Conversely, a
labelled period $T$ combined with the symmetry at zero gives reflection
symmetry about $T/2$, and therefore zero velocity there.  The period $2\tau$
need not be minimal unless $\tau$ is the first positive brake.
\end{proof}

In the center-of-mass frame define Jacobi vectors
\[
X=q_2-q_1,\qquad
Y=q_3-\frac{m_1q_1+m_2q_2}{m_1+m_2}
\]
and Hopf invariants
\[
h(X,Y)=\left(\frac{|X|^2-|Y|^2}{2},X\cdot Y,X\times Y\right).
\]
We use the quotient residual
\[
\Bcal(u,t)=\frac{d}{dt}h(X(u,t),Y(u,t)).
\]
Away from triple collision, $Dh$ has rank three and kernel equal to the common
rotation direction.  Since total linear and angular momentum vanish,
$\Bcal=0$ is equivalent to vanishing of every labelled inertial velocity.
Unlike three mutual-distance derivatives, this remains true at syzygy.

\section{Universal reduced system}

Let
\[
\mu_1=\frac{m_1m_2}{m_1+m_2},\qquad
\mu_2=\frac{m_3(m_1+m_2)}{m_1+m_2+m_3}.
\]
Rotate so that $X=(R,0)$ and write $Y=(P,Q)$.  At zero angular momentum,
Routh reduction gives
\[
K_{red}=\frac{\mu_1\dot R^2+\mu_2(\dot P^2+\dot Q^2)}2
-\frac{\mu_2^2(P\dot Q-Q\dot P)^2}
{2[\mu_1R^2+\mu_2(P^2+Q^2)]}.
\]
The positive potential is
\begin{align*}
U={m_1m_2\over R}
&+{m_1m_3\over\sqrt{(P+{m_2R\over m_1+m_2})^2+Q^2}}\\
&+{m_2m_3\over\sqrt{(P-{m_1R\over m_1+m_2})^2+Q^2}}.
\end{align*}
The Pythagorean curve on the brake boundary is
\[
R=1,\qquad P={AB(B-A)\over A+B},\qquad Q=AB,
\qquad \dot R=\dot P=\dot Q=0.
\]

\section{Exact initial geometry}

The initial center of mass is the incenter because its barycentric weights are
the opposite side lengths.  With $I=\sum m_i|q_i-q_{CM}|^2$ and positive
potential magnitude $U$, direct calculation gives
\[
I_0=abc,\qquad
U_0={ab\over c}+{ac\over b}+{bc\over a}.
\]
Thus after normalization
\[
I_0=AB,\qquad U_0=AB+(AB)^{-1},\qquad H=-U_0.
\]
The configurational measure at fixed masses is
\[
\mu_0=U_0\sqrt{I_0}=(AB)^{3/2}+(AB)^{-1/2},
\]
and decreases strictly on the fundamental interval.  It is not invariant under
the simultaneous mass--length scaling used above.

For $D=r_{12}^2-r_{23}^2-r_{31}^2$, Newton's equations give
\[
D(0)=\dot D(0)=0,\qquad
\ddot D(0)=2\left(\frac1{AB}-AB(A+B)\right)>0.
\]
This is local only.  Converged ordinary numerical experiments show $D$ later
changes sign for $u=1/3$, so a global $D>0$ cone is not pursued.
An exact fourth-order calculation gives
\[
D^{(4)}(0)={P_{20}(u)\over
u^4(u-1)^4(u+1)^2(1+u^2)^4}.
\]
Exact Sturm arithmetic shows that $P_{20}$ has no zero on
$(0,83/200)$ and $P_{20}(0)=-1$.  Hence $D^{(4)}(0)<0$ throughout the
fundamental interval.  This uniform local sign still supplies no global cone.

\section{The real isosceles-right endpoint}

\begin{theorem}
At $u=\sqrt2-1$, the classical solution has no positive second brake and
reaches a binary or triple collision in finite time.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Here $m_1=m_2=m=1/\sqrt2$ and reflection symmetry persists.  Write the base
bodies as $(-x,y_b),(x,y_b)$ and the apex as $(0,y_a)$, with
$h=y_a-y_b$.  Then
\[
\ddot x=-\frac{m}{4x^2}-\frac{x}{(x^2+h^2)^{3/2}}<0.
\]
Starting with $x_0=1/2$ and $\dot x(0)=0$ gives $\dot x<0$, excluding a
second brake.  While $x>0$, one has
$\ddot x\leq-m/(4x_0^2)$, whence
\[
x(t)\leq x_0-\frac{m}{8x_0^2}t^2.
\]
Thus collision occurs no later than $\sqrt{8x_0^3/m}=2^{1/4}$.
\end{proof}

The endpoint is irrational, so this theorem does not settle an integer case.
Its perturbation is singular because the collision unfolds into a close
ordinary encounter when reflection symmetry is broken.

\section{The first skinny encounter}

Put $\epsilon=B$, $A=\sqrt{1-\epsilon^2}$, and $M=A+1$. For the tight-pair
and outer Jacobi vectors
\[
X=q_3-q_1,\qquad Y=q_2-\frac{Aq_1+q_3}{M},
\]
rotate the encounter frame so that $X(0)/\epsilon=1$ and scale
\[
X=\epsilon x,\qquad t=\frac{\epsilon^{3/2}}{\sqrt M}\tau.
\]
The exact inner equation is
\[
x_{\tau\tau}=-\frac{x}{|x|^3}
+\frac{\epsilon^3}{M}\left[
\Phi\left(y-\frac{A\epsilon}{M}x\right)
-\Phi\left(y+\frac{\epsilon}{M}x\right)\right],
\qquad \Phi(v)=\frac{v}{|v|^3}.
\]
The outer acceleration in $\tau$-time is $O(\epsilon^3)$ on a fixed
encounter interval. Introduce Levi--Civita variables
\[
x=z^2,\qquad d\tau=|z|^2ds.
\]
The augmented equations for $z$, $z_s$, the osculating Kepler energy, and the
outer state are real analytic through $z=0$. At $\epsilon=0$,
\[
z_0(s)=\cos(s/\sqrt2),\qquad s_c=\pi/\sqrt2.
\]
The first transverse parameter variation occurs at order five and satisfies
\[
v_{ss}+\frac12v=-\frac38\cos^5(s/\sqrt2),\qquad
v(0)=v_s(0)=0,
\]
so that
\[
v(s_c)=-\frac{15\pi}{128}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[First skinny miss]
There is $\epsilon_0>0$ such that for every real member with
$0<B=\epsilon<\epsilon_0$, the first tight-pair encounter is collision-free.
At closest passage,
\[
r_{13,\min}=\frac{225\pi^2}{16384}\epsilon^{11}(1+O(\epsilon)),
\qquad
h_{13}=-\frac{15\pi}{64}\epsilon^{11/2}
+O(\epsilon^{13/2}),
\]
where $h_{13}=X\times\dot X$ is specific pair angular momentum.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Analytic dependence gives
\[
\operatorname{Im}z(s_c,\epsilon)
=-\frac{15\pi}{128}\epsilon^5+O(\epsilon^6).
\]
Since $\partial_s\operatorname{Re}z_0(s_c)=-1/\sqrt2$, choose a fixed
neighborhood on which the real part remains strictly decreasing. Its unique
zero has the displayed nonzero imaginary part. Outside that neighborhood
$z_0$ is bounded away from zero up to a fixed outgoing section, while both
outer separations are uniformly bounded below. Thus no collision occurs.
Squaring the transverse displacement and converting the scaled angular
momentum to physical time gives the two coefficients.
\end{proof}

The result does not yet prove skinny nonperiodicity. The binary remains bound
with specific energy $-M/\epsilon+O(\epsilon^3)$, and the first-passage outer
radial escape margin is $-2+O(\epsilon)$. The outer body falls back on an
order-one time scale, during which the binary executes
$O(\epsilon^{-3/2})$ cycles. A phase-uniform theorem for the subsequent close
interaction remains open.

\begin{lemma}[Terminal escape certificate]
For a prospective binary of total mass $M$ and an outer body of mass $m_c$,
let $r,\rho$ be the inner and outer Jacobi radii and
$e=|\dot x|^2/2-M/r$. Choose $\eta>0$, set $R=M/\eta$ and
$s_0=\rho_0-R$, and suppose
\[
s_0>0,\quad \dot\rho_0>0,\quad
\delta=\frac12\dot\rho_0^2-\frac{M+m_c}{s_0}>0,
\]
\[
e_0+\frac{m_c\sqrt{2MR}}
{\sqrt{2\delta}\,s_0^2}<-\eta.
\]
Then the future solution either ends in inner binary collision or the outer
body escapes with $\dot\rho\ge\sqrt{2\delta}$ forever. In particular there is
no later classical brake.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
On the bootstrap region $e<-\eta$, one has
$r<R$ and $r|\dot x|<\sqrt{2MR}$. The Newton field derivative bound
$\|D(v/|v|^3)\|\le2/|v|^3$ gives
\[
|\dot e|\le\frac{2m_cr|\dot x|}{(\rho-r)^3}.
\]
Meanwhile
\[
J=\frac12\dot\rho^2-\frac{M+m_c}{\rho-R}
\]
is nondecreasing while $\dot\rho>0$. Hence
$\rho\ge\rho_0+\sqrt{2\delta}(t-t_0)$, and the total future tidal increase of
$e$ is at most the allowance in the hypothesis. Its strict margin closes the
bootstrap.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The late-plunge restricted limit}

The terminal lemma cannot be applied uniformly over the later binary phase.
Indeed, rescale near the outer plunge by
\[
X=\epsilon R,\qquad Y=\epsilon Z,\qquad
t=t_*+\epsilon^{3/2}\theta.
\]
On compact nonsingular sets the exact Jacobi equations tend to
\[
R_{\theta\theta}=-2\frac{R}{|R|^3},\qquad
Z_{\theta\theta}
=-\left[\Phi(Z+R/2)+\Phi(Z-R/2)\right].
\tag{LR}
\]
After regularizing the prescribed binary, this convergence extends through
its binary collisions provided the light particle is separate. The invariant
rectilinear subspace has
\[
R=(r,0),\quad Z=(0,z),\qquad
\ddot z=-\frac{2z}{(z^2+r^2/4)^{3/2}},
\]
where $r$ is the periodic collision--ejection binary with apocenter one.
The outer instantaneous energy obeys
\[
\frac d{d\theta}\left(
\frac12\dot z^2-\frac2{\sqrt{z^2+r^2/4}}\right)
=\frac{r\dot r}{2(z^2+r^2/4)^{3/2}}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Restricted parabolic separatrix]
When the light particle crosses $z=0$ at binary apocenter, there is a launch
speed $v_*>0$ for which it has no light--heavy collision and
\[
z(\theta)\to+\infty,\qquad \dot z(\theta)\to0
\]
as $\theta\to+\infty$. Reflection and time reversal give a
parabolic-to-parabolic scattering orbit.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For positive $z$, $\ddot z<0$. Small launch speeds turn: with
$r=\cos^2\psi$ and $d\theta=\cos^2\psi\,d\psi$, the zero-speed variational
equation becomes, after $\zeta=\cos\psi\,w$,
\[
w_{\psi\psi}+(1+14\sec^2\psi)w=0.
\]
Sturm comparison forces a simple zero before the first binary collision.
Large launch speeds reach $z=1$ while retaining a speed for which
$\dot z^2/2-2/z>0$; this quantity is thereafter nondecreasing.
Let $\mathcal T$ be the nonempty open bounded set of speeds with a finite
turn and put $v_*=\sup\mathcal T$. The boundary solution cannot turn and
cannot have positive limiting speed, since either property is open. It cannot
remain bounded, because the periodic attraction would then force a turn.
\end{proof}

The binary collisions in (LR) are regularized features of an auxiliary limit,
not physical continuations of a positive-$\epsilon$ orbit. Nevertheless the
separatrix proves that a strict phase-uniform escape margin is impossible in
the limiting problem. A finite-$\epsilon$ proof must instead match the
incoming data and determine the separatrix splitting.

\subsection{Reference phase and finite-parameter splitting}

Put $A=\sqrt{1-\epsilon^2}$, $M=1+A$, $N=M+\epsilon$, and let
$\rho_0^2=2A^2/M$ be the exact initial outer Jacobi radius. Replacing the tight
pair by a point mass gives the exact reference clock
\[
\Phi_{\rm ref}
=\frac{\pi}{\epsilon^{3/2}}\sqrt{\frac MN}\,\rho_0^{3/2}
=\pi\epsilon^{-3/2}-\frac\pi4\epsilon^{-1/2}
-\frac{15\pi}{32}\epsilon^{1/2}+O(\epsilon^{3/2}).
\tag{RC}
\]
Moreover
$\Phi_{\rm ref}'\sim-(3\pi/2)\epsilon^{-5/2}$. Thus every open phase arc
has infinitely many reference preimage intervals accumulating at zero, each
containing rational Euclid parameters. This conclusion concerns the reference
clock until a matching theorem is supplied.

That long-cycle step can be proved without finite-eccentricity averaging. Fix
$0<\alpha<1/6$ and stop at physical outer radius
$\rho=\epsilon^\alpha$. Taylor cancellation in the outer Jacobi field gives a
monopole remainder $O(\epsilon^2\rho^{-4})$. Inner-energy and radial
comparison estimates keep $|X|\le2\epsilon$ and give
\[
t_\alpha-t_0(\epsilon^\alpha)=O(\epsilon^{2-3\alpha}).
\]
In LC variables the scaled binary forcing is
$O(\epsilon^{4-3\alpha})$ over regularized length
$O(\epsilon^{-3/2})$. Harmonic-oscillator variation and the exact LC clock
identity yield a regularized mean-anomaly error
$O(\epsilon^{1-3\alpha})$. Hence, unless a prior classical collision has
already ended the orbit, the renormalized incoming intercept satisfies
\[
\operatorname{dist}_{S^1}(\chi_\alpha,\Phi_{\rm ref})
=O(\epsilon^{1/2-3\alpha})=o(1).
\tag{PM}
\]
The remaining matching problem is propagation from this early overlap to one
fixed section of the full restricted incoming parabolic tail.

On the limiting symmetric orbit let $d^2=z^2+r^2/4$. Expansion of the exact
finite-parameter Jacobi field gives
\[
r''=-\frac2{r^2}-\epsilon\frac r{d^3}
+\frac{\epsilon^2}{2r^2}+O(\epsilon^3),
\]
\[
Z_y''=-\frac{2z}{d^3}-\epsilon\frac z{d^3}
+\epsilon^2\frac z{2d^3}+O(\epsilon^3),\qquad
Z_x''=\epsilon^2\frac{3r^3}{16d^5}+O(\epsilon^3).
\tag{FS}
\]
The centered order-$\epsilon$ energy-transfer variation cancels by parity;
longitudinal bending starts at order $\epsilon^2$. The first
symmetry-allowed splitting direction is binary phase. Its naive whole-line
Jacobi field is nevertheless gauge-degenerate: choosing the global
time-translation field $\zeta=z'$ makes the proposed integrand exactly
\[
\frac d{d\theta}\left(\frac{rr'}{2d^3}\right).
\]
Because $z'=O(|\theta|^{-1/3})$ at parabolic infinity, zero limiting data do
not eliminate this mode. A well-posed transversality theorem must instead fix
the parabolic epoch or compare incoming and outgoing parabolic curves
$V_u,V_s$ on $z=0$. Reversibility gives $V_s(\phi)=V_u(-\phi)$, so the target
is $2V_u'(0)\ne0$. Ordinary finite-cutoff calculations support this with a
slope difference near $-0.90623$, but no analytic or validated result is
claimed.

There is a finite sufficient certificate for this transversality. Let
$h''=ah$, $h(0)=v_*$, $h'(0)=0$, with
$a=(4z^2-r^2/2)/(z^2+r^2/4)^{5/2}$. Fixed-softening comparison and an explicit
early escape estimate give
\[
\sqrt8<v_*<4.
\]
If $W=hz''-h'z'$, then
$W'=hbr'$ with $b=3zr/[2(z^2+r^2/4)^{5/2}]$. During the first binary
contraction this proves $(h/z')'>0$ as long as $h>0$, and hence rules out a
first zero of $h$. A force bound also gives
\[
z(\pi/8)>
\frac{\pi\sqrt2}{4}-\frac{2\pi^2}{27\sqrt3}>\frac1{2\sqrt2},
\]
so $a>0$ thereafter. Thus $h'(\pi/4)>0$ forces $h$ to grow forever.

This finite sign is now rigorous. Put $q=h/v$, $w=z'$, $p=q'$, and use
eccentric anomaly $r=\cos^2\psi$, $d\theta=r\,d\psi$. The regular compact
system is
\[
z_\psi=rw,\quad w_\psi=-{2zr\over d^3},\quad
q_\psi=rp,\quad
p_\psi=r{4z^2-r^2/2\over d^5}q,
\tag{CJ}
\]
with $(z,w,q,p)(0)=(0,v,1,0)$ and terminal point $\psi=\pi/2$.

\begin{theorem}[Computer-assisted finite Jacobi sign]
For every $v\in[14/5,4]$, the solution of \emph{(CJ)} exists through
$\psi=\pi/2$ and
\[
p(\pi/2)>\frac1{125}.
\]
In particular, the parabolic-separatrix field satisfies
\[
h'(\pi/4)>\frac{14}{625}>0.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Certificate description]
CAPD 6.1.0 native outward-rounded intervals were used at the pinned commit
\texttt{731079217a9254ea2948d742df2b170895effe7f}. The interval
$[14/5,4]$ was divided into 256 overlapping slabs and each slab was propagated
independently by an order-20 interval Taylor solver with a doubleton set.
Every terminal interval had lower endpoint greater than $1/125$. The complete
verifier prints exact hexadecimal binary64 endpoints; the dependency check,
concise certificate, and symbolic regression test are included in the
repository. Since $14/5<\sqrt8<v_*<4$ and $h=v_*q$, the stated corollary
follows.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Restricted parabolic transversality]
The incoming and outgoing parabolic curves of the rectilinear equal-heavy-mass
restricted problem cross transversely at the reversible apocenter-centered
orbit.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The collision-regularized stable-manifold implication can be checked directly.
Compactify infinity by $z=2/x^2$. In eccentric anomaly the field becomes
\[
x_\psi=-{r\over4}x^3w,\qquad
w_\psi=-{r\over2}x^4(1+r^2x^4/16)^{-3/2}.
\]
It is analytic at $(x,w)=(0,0)$ and through $r=0$. Since
$\int_0^\pi r\,d\psi=\pi/2$, its time-$\pi$ map has, after
$X=x$, $Y=w-\sqrt2x$, the form
\[
X_1=X-\frac\pi8X^3(Y+\sqrt2X)+O_7,qquad
Y_1=Y+{\pi\sqrt2\over8}X^3Y+O_7.
\tag{PM}
\]
Before this shear, $y=w/\sqrt2$ puts the exact map in the factored form
\[
x_1=x-kx^3(y+R_1),\qquad y_1=y-kx^3(x+R_2),
\quad k={\pi\sqrt2\over8},
\]
where analyticity of the exact flow and the $x^3,x^4$ factors in the vector
field make $R_1,R_2$ analytic of order at least four (write $x=x_0u$ to
factor the exact flow by $x_0^3$ and $x_0^4$).
The degree-four term obeys $p_1(X,0)<0$, $p_2(X,0)=0$, and
$\partial_Yp_2(X,0)>0$ for $X>0$. McGehee's theorem
\cite{McGehee1973} therefore gives the fixed-phase parabolic stable curve
$Y=\varphi(X)$ with $\varphi(0)=\varphi'(0)=0$, analytic for $X>0$.
McGehee's graph construction supplies an arbitrarily small Lipschitz constant
on a narrowed sector, hence $|\varphi'|$ is correspondingly small for $X>0$.

Restricting (PM) to this graph gives, for positive constants $c_i$ and small
$X$,
\[
c_1X^4\le X-F(X)\le c_2X^4,qquad
0<F'(X)\le1-c_3X^3.
\]
Thus $X_n^{-3}\asymp n$, $\sum X_n^3=\infty$, and every stable-curve tangent
$\delta X_n=(F^n)'(X_0)\delta X_0$ tends to zero.

Take both section speeds positive in the direction from $z<0$ to $z>0$.
The reversor $\widetilde z(t)=-z(-t)$ then gives
$V_s(\phi)=V_u(-\phi)$. Tangency at zero would force both slopes to vanish.
In extended variables $(\delta z,\delta w,\delta\phi)$, the global time-shift
and pinned stable-phase tangents are explicitly
\[
T_{\rm time}=(v_*,0,1),\qquad T_{\rm pin}=(0,V_s'(0),1)=(0,0,1).
\]
Their difference is the fixed-phase stable tangent with $z$-component
$h=z'-\eta_{\rm pin}$. The
validated theorem and $a>0$ imply
$h(\theta)\ge h(\pi/4)+(14/625)(\theta-\pi/4)$. But
\[
\delta X_n=-{X_n^3\over4}h(\theta_n),qquad
\theta_n=\theta_0+n\pi/2,
\]
so $X_n^{-3}\asymp n$ makes $\liminf|\delta X_n|>0$, contradicting tangent
contraction.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Restricted phase windows]
There are one-sided open binary-phase intervals adjacent to the centered
separatrix such that the incoming parabolic orbit escapes hyperbolically on
one interval and turns at finite positive $z$ on the other.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The analytic section difference
$\Delta(\phi)=V_u(\phi)-V_s(\phi)$ is odd and has
$\Delta'(0)\ne0$. It therefore has a strict linear sign on the two sides of
zero. McGehee's stable curve separates the local hyperbolic and returning
sectors. On the returning side $z''<0$ for $z>0$; any trajectory that neither
turns nor escapes with positive speed would be another parabolic point on the
unique stable curve. Thus it turns in finite time.
\end{proof}

Parameterize the restricted incoming parabolic curve by its asymptotic phase
intercept $\chi$, normalized by
$\chi=\lim_{Y\to\infty}(\phi_Y+(4/3)Y^{3/2})$ modulo $2\pi$. This coordinate
includes the finite restricted quadrupole correction between infinity and a
fixed section; a raw monopole section phase does not. Consequently, if the
true full regularized state on a fixed incoming section converges to the
restricted state with intercept $\Phi_{\rm ref}(B)$, infinitely many open
terminal nonperiodicity intervals accumulating at $B=0$ contain rational
Euclid parameters. Strict restricted escape on a compact intercept arc
transfers to a finite outgoing scaled section; there
$e=-C/B+o(B^{-1})$ and $\dot\rho=sB^{-1/2}+o(B^{-1/2})$, so the terminal
escape lemma applies uniformly, yielding escape or an earlier classical inner
collision and excluding a later brake in either case. This is a rigorous
conditional implication; the uniform matching hypothesis remains open.

\section{Cardinality and the remaining obstruction}

The rational parameters are countable and dense.  Neither countability,
measure zero, analyticity, nor the nominal three-equations/two-unknowns count
excludes rational zeros.  A proof still requires either no real zero, an exact
arithmetic characterization of all real zeros, or a finite exact reduction.

The active route factors a possible brake, away from Jacobi degeneracies, into
two radial-rate equations followed by an angular-slip equation, and seeks to
classify the isolated double-radial events along the first-syzygy continuation.
Ordinary scans show both signs of angular slip on different double-radial
events, ruling out a universal fixed-sign shortcut. The skinny fallback has
now resolved the first limiting collision exactly, but uniform control through
the later phase-sensitive plunge is open.

\section{Necessary integral identities}

For a hypothetical second brake at $\tau$, the Lagrange--Jacobi identity is
\[
\ddot I=4K-2U=2U-4U_0.
\]
Both endpoint dilational momenta vanish, so
\[
\int_0^\tau Udt=2U_0\tau,\qquad
\int_0^\tau Kdt=U_0\tau.
\]
Integrating each labelled acceleration also gives zero.  If
$d_{ij}=q_j-q_i$, multiplication by the fixed initial positions yields the
additional scalar condition
\[
\int_0^\tau\sum_{i<j}m_im_j
{d_{ij}(0)\cdot d_{ij}(t)\over r_{ij}(t)^3}dt=0.
\]
Its integrand is initially positive, but no global projection inequality has
been proved through close encounters.

At energy $-U_0$, the Jacobi--Maupertuis metric is
$2(U-U_0)ds_m^2$.  A second-brake segment is a degenerate-boundary geodesic
chord.  A Morse-index or convexity obstruction remains open.

\section{Catalog comparison}

Li and Liao list a nearby periodic brake orbit on the mass slice
$(0.6,0.8,1)$.  The exact tied point is $(x,y)=(0.14,0.48)$, while their
closest listed entry is $(0.1446319096,0.4773197126)$.  Its side lengths and
right-triangle defect do not satisfy the exact equations.  Equal-mass modern
catalogs are also disjoint from the tied right-triangle curve.  No inspected
catalog supplies a validated exact counterexample.  Local shooting continuation
of the $F_{30}$ family varies its masses and reduces the tied-shape gap to about
$0.00369$, but the two shape-matching coordinates cross zero at distinct mass
parameters.  This is ordinary numerical evidence, not validated
nonintersection.

\section{Open proof obligations}

The rational and strong real conjectures remain unresolved.  The missing
theorem must control arbitrarily close collision-free encounters, classify or
exclude every double-radial event including Jacobi degeneracies, and be uniform
at parameter endpoints.  If real intersections remain, an exact arithmetic
bridge is still required.
